---
title: "Assignment engine: how claims are routed to technicians"
description: "Learn how the assignment engine picks a technician: least-loaded for URGENT claims, most-experienced for CRITICAL claims, filtered by claim type and product."
---

When a claim is registered or transferred, the system automatically selects a `TechnicalEmployee` to handle it. You never specify a technician manually; instead, the engine applies one of two selection strategies depending on the claim's `classification`. This keeps workloads balanced for routine faults and routes the most demanding cases to the most proven expert.

## Eligibility criteria

Before any ranking strategy is applied, the engine builds a pool of eligible technicians. A technician is eligible only if they are configured as an expert in **both**:

- the **claim type** — matched against `TechnicalEmployee.claimTypes` (ManyToMany)
- the **product** on the affected contract — matched against `TechnicalEmployee.products` (ManyToMany)

The repository query is:

```java
technicalEmployeeRepository.findByClaimTypesIdAndProductsId(
    claim.getClaimType().getId(),
    claim.getContract().getProduct().getId()
);
```

A technician who handles "Cable Dañado" faults but is not configured for "Internet" will not be considered for a cable claim on an Internet contract, even if they are otherwise available.

## Urgent claims — least loaded

For claims classified as `URGENT`, the engine selects the eligible technician with the **fewest currently assigned (pending) claims**.

```java
employees.stream()
    .min(Comparator.comparingInt(TechnicalEmployee::getAssignedClaimsCount))
    .orElseThrow(...);
```

`getAssignedClaimsCount()` returns the size of `claimsAssigned` — the list of claims the technician has accepted but not yet resolved.

<Note>
The least-loaded strategy distributes urgent faults evenly across the available team. No single technician accumulates a backlog while others sit idle.
</Note>

## Critical claims — most experienced

For claims classified as `CRITICAL`, the engine selects the eligible technician with the **most resolved claims** in their history.

```java
employees.stream()
    .max(Comparator.comparingInt(TechnicalEmployee::getResolvedClaimsCount))
    .orElseThrow(...);
```

`getResolvedClaimsCount()` returns the size of `claimsResolved` — the cumulative count of all claims this technician has successfully closed.

<Note>
The most-experienced strategy routes critical faults to the technician with the strongest track record. A technician who has resolved many claims of the same type and product is statistically more likely to resolve a difficult new one efficiently.
</Note>

## Transfer re-assignment

When a `TechnicalEmployee` requests a transfer (`POST /api/claims/claim-transfer`), the system calls the same assignment engine but **excludes the requesting technician** from the eligible pool:

```java
experts = experts.stream()
    .filter(e -> !e.equals(exclude))
    .toList();
```

The same classification-based strategy (least-loaded for `URGENT`, most-experienced for `CRITICAL`) is then applied to the remaining eligible technicians.

<Warning>
If no eligible technician is found — either because no technician is configured for the required claim type and product combination, or because the only eligible technician is the one being excluded — the assignment will throw a `RuntimeException` and the operation will fail. Ensure your technical employees are configured with appropriate `claimTypes` and `products` before registering claims that depend on them.
</Warning>
